01Context and problem

Household money is scattered. Nobody sees the whole month.

Rent goes out in one app. Electricity and gas are paid in another. Flatmates sort out the rest on chat.

TrackIt is an iOS app for couples, families and flatmates in India. It started as one place to pay every recurring bill. This version makes the household the centre: bills, shared expenses, budgets, payments and spending insights on one monthly cycle.

What TrackIt is, and what it is not

A household finance app

It organises what a household owes, who pays and where the money goes. Payments are handed off to a UPI app people already trust. It does not replace Google Pay.

Two proposed integrations

Bank transactions can be imported through a consent-based Account Aggregator, and payments handed off to a UPI app. Both are designed as screens. Neither is built.

What went wrong for households

Problem 01

Bills all over the place

Recurring payments live across several apps, so nothing shows the full month.

Problem 02

Paying one by one is hectic

Eleven bills means eleven separate payments every month.

Problem 03

Due dates get missed

With no reminder in one place, a bill is forgotten until it's overdue.

Problem 04

Auto-pay without a ceiling

People understand auto-pay but won't use it, because they can't cap it.

Problem 05

Shared costs in chats

Flatmates settle rent and groceries over messages, so nobody trusts the balance.

Problem 06

Failures are a black box

When one bill in a batch fails, people can't tell what was taken and what to do next.

Problem statement

Households need one monthly system where they can see what they owe, coordinate who pays, split shared costs and understand where their money goes, without giving up control of it.

The patternEvery app is built around a single transaction. None treats this month's bills as one thing you can review, authorise and close in one pass.

6 insights that shaped TrackIt

Insight 01

The problem is the calendar, not the payment.

Nobody struggles to pay one bill. They struggle to remember which one is due this week.

One monthly basket, sorted by due date

Insight 02

Auto-pay fails on trust, not on setup.

One unexpected spike and the money is gone before they see it.

Approval for spikes; any bill can be held back

Insight 03

Proof matters as much as payment.

A payment without a receipt people can find is not finished.

A result and a receipt for every bill

Insight 04

Wallets are a dead end.

People won't park a month of household money in a wallet.

Pay straight from the bank account

Insight 05

Shared money needs one record.

Splits, budgets and bills kept apart never add up.

Bills, splits and budgets on one record

Insight 06

Help with the tedious parts, never the money.

People wanted help, and refused anything that acted on its own.

An assistant that drafts and waits

The tensionPeople say they want automation, and feel unsafe handing over control. That tension became the central design problem.

No rolesA household has no manager. Every member can add bills, expenses and splits. A bank connection and its consent belong to the person who gave it.

04Key decisions

The household is the unit. The assistant suggests, and the user decides.

Decision 01

Make the household the unit

Instead of
Treating every transaction as one person’s expense
I chose
A household workspace: shared bills, a responsible person for each bill, and expense splits
Because
Household finances involve several people. The person who pays is not always the person who owes.
Decision 02

One monthly payment basket

Instead of
Opening every bill and every payment app one by one
I chose
A basket of the month’s bills. Pick the ones to pay now, check the total, then pay from a UPI app
Because
The monthly cycle is how a household really pays. The basket does not promise one combined payment, and the screens say so.
Decision 03

Bank data is optional, and checked by the user

Instead of
Depending on one data source
I chose
Optional bank import with a consent screen, a review of imported categories, manual entry and statement upload
Because
Bank descriptions can be ambiguous, and not everyone will connect a bank on day one.
Decision 04

Keep AI advisory, not autonomous

Instead of
An assistant that decides budgets and splits
I chose
Suggestions the user can read, edit and confirm. Facts from records and suggestions are labelled apart
Because
Budgets and splits affect real household money, so a person confirms every one.
Decision 05

Explain overspending with triggers, not only totals

Instead of
A monthly total and a category chart
I chose
Day and time patterns, the exact purchases, and plans to avoid each trigger, all inside Insights
Because
A total says what was spent. It does not say when or why.

06The product

One household, from setup to next month's plan.

Every screen uses one sample household: a flat in Koramangala, Bengaluru, with Aarav, Riya and Ankit, and bills like rent, BESCOM, Jio, Netflix, Tata Play and Indane gas.

01

Set up the household and connect a bank

Create a household, invite people, and optionally connect a bank. The purpose is explained before any consent is asked for.

  • Bank connection is optional and can be skipped
  • Consent shows what is shared, why, for how long and that it cannot move money
  • Imported categories are reviewed by the user, with duplicates flagged
02

Add and manage bills

Rent repeats. A repair happens once. Electricity changes every month. The bill form handles all three without piling up fields.

  • Recurring and one-time are one switch
  • A variable bill asks the user to confirm each month’s amount
  • A possible duplicate is flagged before saving
  • A bill paid outside TrackIt can be marked as paid
Solves insight 01
03

Pay the monthly basket

The basket is the centre of the product: review what is due, choose what to pay now, and follow each payment to its result.

  • Each bill is paid in a UPI app the user already trusts
  • Waiting for the bank is shown as waiting, never as failed
  • One screen per state: success, pending, partial and not installed
Solves insights 01 and 03
04

Split shared bills and expenses

Rent repeats every month. A dinner happens once. They are split in different places, and a settlement is never counted as spending.

  • Shared bills and one-time expenses sit under two switches
  • Equal is the default. Custom and percentage appear when needed
  • Split with AI reads a receipt and asks who had what
Solves insight 05
05

Split a receipt with AI

Scan a receipt, say who shared it in plain words, and review every share. The assistant asks when it cannot know who had an item.

  • Extraction is a draft the user can correct
  • It never assumes who consumed an item
  • Shares always add up to the receipt
Solves insight 06
06

See when and why spending goes up

Insights adds the date and time. A heatmap shows when the household spends most, a calendar shows the days over the daily pace, and each trigger lists its purchases.

  • Facts from records are tagged apart from suggestions
  • Each plan is a suggestion that becomes an ordinary budget setting only when the user taps it
  • If only one account is connected, Insights says it is based on partial data
07

Plan next month with AI

The planner uses real spending, explains each number and lets the user edit it. When a target cannot be met, it offers choices instead of cutting essentials.

  • Marked as a draft until the user confirms
  • When the numbers conflict, one option is Suggest what to cut: shopping, entertainment and dining first
  • It says how much is still short, and does not cut groceries to close the gap
Solves insight 06

Profile: where consent lives

Bank connections and consent

Each connection shows when it last imported and when its consent ends. Revoking stops new imports straight away.

UPI preferences and mandates

A default UPI app, and the autopay mandates. Mandates are changed in the bank app, never by TrackIt.

07Edge cases and trade-offs

An unknown payment is not a failed one. Every state says what happened and what to do.

I did not give every edge case its own flow. The important ones sit beside the main flow they belong to.

Why it mattersA payment with no confirmation yet could still have been charged. Showing it as failed would invite a second payment. So pending is its own state, and retry stays locked until the bank answers.

The states, on screen

Trade-offs I made

Trade-off 01

Import or type

Importing makes setup easier but depends on supported banks and consent. Manual entry gives control but takes effort. Both stay.

Trade-off 02

A basket, not a bulk payment

The basket unifies review and tracking. It does not promise one combined UPI payment, because each payment may need its own approval.

Trade-off 03

Flexible splits, equal first

Real households split unevenly. Equal is the default, and custom or percentage appear only when asked for.

Trade-off 04

Confirmation adds steps

Asking the user to confirm every AI result adds a tap. It keeps the user in control of real money.

What I learned

  1. 01

    Start from the household

    A bank transaction does not say who in the household should bear it.

  2. 02

    Unknown is not failed

    A payment with no answer yet could still have gone through.

  3. 03

    Say what is proposed

    Integrations that are not built should be labelled, not implied.

  4. 04

    Totals say what, triggers say when

    Knowing the day and time of overspending is what makes a plan possible.
